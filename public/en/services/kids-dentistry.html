Common signs

When should you come in?

  • Small cavities, dark spots or stains on your child's teeth
  • A toothache, or swelling and pus in the gums or cheek
  • Around age 6, a double row of teeth: the permanent tooth is in but the baby tooth hasn't fallen out
  • Front teeth biting backward, or new teeth coming in crooked
  • It's been a while since the last checkup, and there's a lot of white plaque

These are common signs only. A dentist needs to examine you to diagnose the problem and recommend treatment.

Why bring your child in early?

Baby teeth need care and regular checkups too, so cavities, gum inflammation and jaw or facial development issues are caught early as your child grows. Don't wait for a toothache to bring your child in.

A small cavity left too long, especially over Tet or summer break when kids eat more sweets, can turn into pulp inflammation, pain and bigger bills. Losing a baby molar early can make nearby teeth tilt into the gap and throw off the bite. Remind your child to brush after meals and before bed.

FAQ

Questions about your child's teeth

Does pulling a baby tooth need numbing?

Yes. No matter how easy the tooth is to pull, it should be fully numbed so your child isn't in pain and doesn't grow up afraid of the dentist. The dentist applies numbing gel first, then gives the injection.

My 6-year-old has a double row of teeth. Does the baby tooth need to come out?

If the permanent tooth is in but the baby tooth hasn't fallen out, the new tooth may come in crooked or crowded. Bring your child in so the dentist can check whether the baby tooth should come out. Once there's enough room, the permanent tooth usually moves into place on its own.

How do you fill cavities for young kids who won't sit still?

For young kids with several cavities, the dentist may apply SDF and place Fuji fillings: quick and simple, and they help keep the decay from getting worse. Applying SDF takes only about 3-5 minutes.
