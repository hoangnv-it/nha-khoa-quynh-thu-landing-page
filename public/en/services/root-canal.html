Common signs

When should you come in?

  • A toothache that gets worse at night and keeps you from eating or sleeping
  • A large cavity, or a badly broken, dark tooth
  • A front tooth that has darkened from long-term pulp inflammation
  • Swelling or pus in the gums or cheek around the root
  • A tooth broken in an accident or fall
  • A tooth that had a root canal but hurts again

These are common signs only. A dentist needs to examine you to diagnose the problem and recommend treatment.

Saving your natural teeth comes first

Modern dentistry aims to preserve as much of the natural tooth as possible. At Quynh Thu Dental, the dentist tries to save your tooth whenever possible: treating the root canal, then restoring the tooth with a porcelain crown, a fiber post or bonded porcelain instead of pulling it.

For a broken tooth with exposed pulp, the dentist may be able to preserve the pulp: removing only the exposed part, keeping the pulp in the root, placing medication and checking again in about 2 weeks.

FAQ

Questions about root canals

How many visits does a root canal take?

Many cases are done in 1-2 visits. If there's infection or swelling, the dentist places medication and sees you again in about 1-2 weeks.

Do I need a crown after a root canal?

If a lot of the tooth is gone after a root canal, it's usually restored with a porcelain crown, a fiber post or bonded porcelain, so you can chew normally and keep your natural root. The dentist will advise you tooth by tooth.

What do you use for fillings?

We clean out the decay and fill the tooth with composite: smooth and matched to your natural tooth color. We also do cosmetic bonding for small gaps and worn spots near the gumline.
